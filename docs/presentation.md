## Slide 3 — Problem Statement

**The real-life problem**
- Students find out they are struggling only when results come out. It is too late then
- Students don't know how study hours and attendance affect their result
- Teachers can't track every student in a large class

**Our solution**
- Students: check expected marks, pass chance and tips
- Teachers: see their own class and who needs support first

**Speaker notes:**
The problem is simple. Most students only find out they are in trouble when the results come. Before that, they are guessing: am I studying enough, does my attendance matter? Teachers also cannot watch every student in a big class, so a student who is slipping is noticed late. Support after a failed exam is much less useful than support before it. My project is an early warning and self-check website. A student enters study hours, attendance and last marks, and gets expected marks, a pass chance and tips. A teacher gets a class code, students join with it, and the teacher sees their own students with the ones who need support listed first.

## Slide 8 — Data Preprocessing

- Load CSV with pandas
- Remove missing, duplicate and invalid rows
- Validate ranges (hours 0–24, % and marks 0–100)
- Inputs: study hours, attendance, previous marks
- 80/20 train-test split, `random_state=42`
- **No data leakage:** exam marks are never used as an input

**Speaker notes:**
First I load and clean the data, removing missing, duplicate and out-of-range rows. The inputs are study hours, attendance and previous marks. I split the data 80 percent for training and 20 percent for testing, with a fixed random state so results are repeatable. Importantly, exam marks are never used as an input, because that would give the model the answer. That is called data leakage.

## Slide 9 — Machine Learning Models

**Linear Regression** → predicts exam **marks**
- marks = intercept + a·hours + b·attendance + c·previous

**Logistic Regression** → predicts **Pass / Fail**
- gives probability of passing; ≥ 0.5 = Pass
- uses scaling inside a pipeline

**Speaker notes:**
Linear Regression predicts a number, the expected marks, by learning how much each input adds. Logistic Regression is used for classification. It calculates the probability that the student passes, and if it is 50 percent or more, we say Pass. I scale the inputs only for logistic regression, because it works better when inputs are on a similar scale.

**Speaker notes:**
These are the real results from my code, calculated on test data the model has never seen.
- Accuracy: out of all students, how many predictions were correct.
- Precision: when the model says Pass, how often it is right.
- Recall: out of students who really passed, how many the model found.
- F1 Score: one number that balances precision and recall.
- R² Score: how much of the variation in marks the model explains; 1 is perfect.
- MAE: on average, how many marks the prediction is wrong by.
The first four are for classification, the last two are for regression. These numbers depend on the dataset and the split, so they would change with different data.
